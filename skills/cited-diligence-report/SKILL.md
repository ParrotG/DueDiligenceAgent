# Cited diligence report

- Start with the decision or concern the user provided. Organize the main findings around what materially changes that decision.
- Keep dates, entities, currencies and measurement definitions attached to the claims they qualify. Cite direct public sources close to important claims and include document locations where possible.
- Use chronology or comparisons when they explain a risk. Keep background in context and remove incidental detail. Preserve contradictions, scope limits and unresolved adverse claims that affect the conclusion.
- Test whether a reader can tell what happened, why it matters and what remains uncertain. A targeted edit should preserve unrelated sections and citations.
